What Google’s Connected Apps can do for U.S. grocery orders

By Evan · Published Nov 25, 2026 · Updated Nov 25, 2026

Vendor announcement Nov 20, 2026

Google announced on November 20 that it was rolling out grocery Connected Apps in the United States through Search and the Gemini app. The announcement names Albertsons, Safeway, Shaw’s and Target. If one of those options is available to you, it can help you put together an order and check out.

Google’s November 20 announcement

A holiday grocery list

Imagine you’re bringing a snack board to a Thanksgiving gathering. Before opening an app, write down the quantity, your deadline and anything the guests need to avoid. If you can use a Connected App, use the list as your starting point and review the items the retailer shows before checkout. For instance, check whether the cracker box is large enough and whether a suggested substitution fits your guests’ needs. Read the retailer’s final total and delivery or pickup details, where offered, before placing the order.

What shoppers and store owners can take from the announcement

Google says the apps are powered by the Universal Commerce Protocol. The announcement concerns a U.S. rollout with named retailers. Availability can therefore start with a simple question for shoppers: does one of those options appear in the Google experience you’re using?

  • Google’s November 20 announcement

If you run an independent shop, look at Google’s merchant documentation for the routes open to your business. Check what a customer would need when moving a shopping list into a cart: pack sizes, availability for the right product variant, and fulfillment terms. Those are things a customer can act on today, irrespective of which shopping interface they use.
