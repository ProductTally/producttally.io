How to find GEO content gaps with Bing AI Performance

Use Bing Webmaster Tools’ Topics Portfolio to inspect citation themes and opportunity queries, then decide whether to improve an existing guide or write a genuinely useful new one. The feature is in beta and its metrics are directional.

By Evan · Updated Nov 10, 2026

On this page

  2. Check whether the report is available for your site
  3. Read the three topic signals without over-interpreting them

Quick answer

Verify your site in Bing Webmaster Tools, open AI Performance, and review Topics Portfolio if enough citation data is available. Use topic size, sites cited, and opportunity queries to find a reader question your site does not answer well. Validate that question before changing a page; the beta report is a planning signal, not a ranking or traffic forecast.

Check whether the report is available for your site

Add and verify the site property in Bing Webmaster Tools, then open AI Performance. Topics Portfolio is a beta view and may not appear when a site has too little activity in AI answers. If the tab is absent, keep using the Citations & Pages view and revisit later; do not infer that the site has no visibility from a missing beta panel.

Read the three topic signals without over-interpreting them

Topic Size estimates citations across sites for a theme; Sites Cited approximates how many other sites appear; Queries counts distinct grounding queries in the cluster. These values describe an ecosystem, not your Google rank, visits, or conversion rate. Bing says the figures are approximate and classifications are generated automatically, so treat small differences as clues to inspect rather than exact measurements.

Turn an opportunity query into an editorial decision

Open the topic and compare Grounding Queries, where your site already appears, with Opportunity Queries, where other sites appear and yours does not. Choose one query and write down the job behind it: learn a concept, compare choices, solve a setup problem, or evaluate a workflow. Search the wording and its variants yourself. If an existing page already serves that job, improve its evidence and clarity; create a new page only when it has a distinct purpose and information you can support.

Improve the page around the question, not the dashboard label

Put a direct answer near the top, then show steps, examples, exceptions, and primary sources. State what you observed, the date and limits of the evidence. Link to a related guide only when it helps the reader continue. Avoid publishing a page for every query variation, adding repeated keyword blocks, or manufacturing mentions: those actions do not supply the missing expertise or make a citation predictable.

Keep a small, repeatable measurement log

Record the date, selected one-, two-, or three-month window, topic, query, cited page, and change made. Recheck the same window and page after it has had time to be crawled, and compare the direction rather than expecting a guaranteed citation. Track Bing AI citations separately from Google Search Console impressions, referral sessions, and signups; each describes a different part of discovery.

Know when this method is not enough

A beta view can omit small topics, group a query unexpectedly, and show approximate opportunity volumes. It is not a universal keyword database. Use reader interviews, support questions, search performance, and your own content evidence to decide whether a topic is worth covering. If the opportunity would require claims you cannot verify, leave it unanswered until you can gather better evidence.
