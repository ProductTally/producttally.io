ChatGPT search and AI training in robots.txt: which settings control each?

Check OAI-SearchBot and GPTBot separately, and see where the proposed shared training preference fits.

By Evan · Updated Nov 12, 2026

On this page

  1. Quick answer
  2. Decide what you want each crawler to do
  3. Read the file your site actually serves
  4. Keep the draft and the live controls separate
  5. Keep a small audit record

Quick answer

OpenAI documents separate controls for search and potential foundation-model training. OAI-SearchBot controls crawling for ChatGPT search. GPTBot handles crawling for potential training use. The common training signal announced October 30 was a future implementation commitment. Use current bot controls for decisions today.

Decide what you want each crawler to do

For a public editorial site that wants search eligibility but declines training crawling, the two groups express different choices. In the OAI-SearchBot group allow the intended public paths. In the GPTBot group disallow the paths you wish to exclude from training crawling. Keep those decisions separate for each production hostname. Search crawl permission only establishes one condition for eligibility.

Read the file your site actually serves

Open /robots.txt at the canonical hostname and confirm it is the intended file, reachable without sign-in. Review the named groups, any wildcard group, and the rules for one public guide and one restricted path. Check that the CDN or firewall does not block the verified search crawler if you want it to fetch public pages. Private content needs access controls regardless of crawler preferences.

Keep the draft and the live controls separate

OpenAI said it intends to honor a common voluntary signal and would share implementation details later. The IETF document is an Internet-Draft. Keep your existing bot-specific controls and treat any common syntax as pending until the provider documents that it honors it. Record the version of the guidance so you can review changes later.

Keep a small audit record

Record the host, intended search and training policies, two sample paths, and the checked date. After deploying a change, recheck the public file and any available crawler logs. Crawling, inclusion in search, and citations are separate observations.
