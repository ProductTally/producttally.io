Udemy course, Personal Plan or Business Team: what to check before paying

Open the relevant course pages and compare exact access, local checkout price and who manages seats.

Quick answer

Buy one Udemy course when you have a specific course you want to revisit. Compare Personal Plan when several courses you will take are included in your account’s catalog. Consider Business Team when the organization needs to manage seats and can verify the exact courses in its separate catalog.

One skill: open the actual course page

Say you need one Premiere Pro course to finish a 30-second edit. Open the exact course, preview the relevant lesson, and check teaching language, the one-time price and the access terms. Udemy generally describes individual purchases as lifetime access to that course, subject to its terms. Budget for the individual purchase if this is the only class you need.

Several skills: check each course in your account

For example, compare your exact editing, captioning and analytics courses. On each course page while signed in, check whether the lesson set is included in Personal Plan in your market. If a course is missing, add its one-time price to your comparison. Use the local renewal price and the number of billing periods you realistically need. Access to the subscription catalog lasts while subscribed.

A team: open the Business plan and confirm the catalog

For three creators who need centralized seats, open Business Plans and check Team eligibility and the current quote. The US page showed 2–50 people at $360 per user billed annually on November 16, 2026, so three seats are $1,080 before applicable taxes. Verify those exact courses in the Business catalog and confirm the admin features available in your market.

Put the quotes into one comparison

Use the checklist to compare one-time course costs, subscription renewals and all needed annual seats. Match one concrete output to each course, such as a finished vertical video or report. This guide uses public plan documentation and an illustrative three-seat calculation.
