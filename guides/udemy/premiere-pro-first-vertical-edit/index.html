How to choose a Udemy Premiere Pro course for your first vertical video

Use one 30-second edit to compare Udemy courses on project setup, reframing, audio and export.

Quick answer

Look for a Udemy Premiere Pro course that walks through an entire edit from raw clips to a file you can watch on a phone. To compare courses, try a fictional 30-second vertical video. Check how the lessons handle framing, audio and export alongside timeline shortcuts.

Udemy Review

Start with a short brief and three clips

Record an opening statement, a demonstration and a closing line. Plan roughly 5, 20 and 5 seconds. Create a 9:16 sequence, such as 1080 × 1920. Trim pauses and align the cuts with the spoken ideas. In the demonstration, keep the object and your hands in frame. Auto Reframe can be a starting point for a horizontal source; play through the result and adjust shots that crop out important action.

Export a file and check it on a phone

Listen for jumps in volume across cuts and make sure background music does not mask speech. If captions are required, correct names and numbers. Decide whether to burn the captions into the video or provide a separate file, depending on the destination. Export, open the resulting file on a phone and check that the first sentence, demonstration and close all survive. Keep the original clips and project so you can revise it later.

Choose a course with a project you can finish

Search Udemy for “Premiere Pro beginner vertical video.” Prefer a course whose preview shows a timeline edit and whose curriculum covers audio and export. Confirm the Premiere version and whether practice footage is supplied. If you routinely deliver short videos, a course that lets you finish and review a small project is easier to judge than a long list of effects.
