Claude Sheets vs Excel for a small team’s content calendar

Compare Claude Sheets beta and Excel with a two-tab calendar, a status formula, and a handoff to a teammate.

By Evan · Updated Nov 12, 2026

On this page

  1. Quick answer
  2. Give both tools the same calendar
  3. Check what a teammate receives
  4. Decide which workbook should be authoritative
  5. Record the test before migrating

Quick answer

Try Claude Sheets when you want to draft and revise a small workbook in conversation. Keep the team’s calendar in Excel if it relies on Power Query, macros, or protected ranges. Use the same fictional calendar in both and check the formula, sharing, and XLSX export before moving live work.

Give both tools the same calendar

Create a Plan tab with Title, Owner, Status, and Due date. Add three rows: a newsletter marked Ready, a product guide marked Draft, and a video marked Ready. On a Summary tab, count Ready rows with COUNTIF(Plan!C2:C4,"Ready"). The expected result is 2. Change the video to Draft and check that it becomes 1. Reuse the same rows and labels in both tools.

Check what a teammate receives

Share the Claude artifact with a colleague and ask them to update one status. In Excel, use a supported workbook in OneDrive or SharePoint Online and grant edit access. Confirm both people see the same value. Export Claude Sheets as XLSX, reopen it in Excel and repeat the status change. Inspect whether the cell contains a working formula rather than only the previous displayed result.

Decide which workbook should be authoritative

The Claude help center documents tabs, cross-sheet formulas, XLSX and CSV export, and shared editing. At launch it lists pivot tables and protected ranges as unavailable, along with Excel macros and Power Query. A plain CSV is useful for moving rows. Choose XLSX when the receiving teammate needs multiple tabs and formulas, and verify them in the target app. Keep the workbook that satisfies your handoff and access requirements as the source of truth.

Record the test before migrating

Note which account and app you used, the two expected counts, whether editing reached the second user, and what survived export. This is a proposed acceptance test based on official documentation. Results depend on the workbook, account, and beta availability.
