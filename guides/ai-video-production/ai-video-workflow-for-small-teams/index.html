AI video workflow for small teams: brief, generate, edit, verify

A practical AI video workflow for creators and small teams. Upwork’s September 2026 U.S. marketplace report lists “AI video” as its top AI-related search; this is not global Google search volume.

Quick answer

Treat AI video as a production workflow, not a one-click export. Write a short brief, generate and review clips shot by shot, edit them around one clear story, then verify facts, sound, captions, rights, and disclosure requirements before publishing.

Write a brief before opening a generator

Name one audience, one outcome, the publishing channel, aspect ratio, approximate duration, and the action viewers should take. List factual claims and visual assets that need permission. A one-page brief makes it easier to compare tools and prevents attractive but unusable footage.

Generate a sequence of reviewable shots

Break the idea into visual beats. Give each shot one subject and action, keep reference images and character descriptions consistent, and save prompt and version notes. Review a few representative shots before generating the rest; this exposes style, motion, and identity problems early.

Edit for continuity and check the finished file

Build the story on a timeline instead of stitching together unrelated generations. Check names, numbers, logos, hands, scene transitions, voice, music rights, caption timing, and mobile safe areas. Play the exported file from start to finish on a phone; a successful render alone does not prove it is ready.

Apply the destination platform’s disclosure rules

For YouTube, disclose realistic AI-generated or meaningfully altered content that depicts real people, places, or events in a way that could be mistaken for real. YouTube says ordinary production assistance and clearly fantastical content do not by themselves require that disclosure. Check the rules of every platform where you repost.

Give each important video a page of its own

Google recommends a dedicated watch page when watching one video is the main reason to visit. Keep the video discoverable in rendered HTML, use a stable thumbnail URL, and write a unique title and description. Add a transcript or concise summary for readers. Video metadata and sitemaps can support discovery, but do not guarantee a video result.

Example: review a 20-second vertical cut

Storyboard three beats: show the problem, demonstrate one action, then show the result. Keep an approved screenshot for the demonstration if a generated shot changes the interface. Check the captions against the script, then watch the export on a phone with sound off. Accept it only if the action and result are still clear.
