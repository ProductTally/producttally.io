How to make a 15-second reference video with Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash

Use your own reference image, plan three beats, and check product details after each generation or edit.

By Evan · Updated Nov 12, 2026

On this page

  1. Quick answer
  2. Plan three beats before generating
  3. Make one local change
  4. Check four frames and one audio pass
  5. Budget for usable footage

Quick answer

Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash supports native clips up to 15 seconds and follow-up edits, according to Google’s November 5 announcement. Start with an image you can use, design three simple beats and compare the output with the reference at several points. Verify your account’s access and supported controls before planning delivery.

Plan three beats before generating

Use a product photo you own, for example an unbranded notebook on a plain background. Make the first five seconds a stable introduction, the next five a slow move toward the cover, and the last five a stable closing shot. Specify the intended aspect ratio, lighting and the features that should stay unchanged, such as cover color, corner shape and spiral placement.

Make one local change

If the first clip gets the object right but misses the framing, ask for a tighter shot during the middle beat and keep the rest of the brief fixed. Compare both versions at the same timestamps. Google documents conversational edits, so test one change at a time. Use a separate editing tool for captions or packaging text that must stay exact.

Check four frames and one audio pass

Inspect the first frame, roughly five and ten seconds, and the last frame. Look for shape drift, incorrect labels, sudden shifts in color, and a closing frame that cuts off too early. Play through once with sound and check for unwanted audio. Keep the source, prompt, version, and reasons for rejecting a take.

Budget for usable footage

Count draft generations, edits and any upscale separately. If you use the API, check the current resolution and duration prices before starting. This is a proposed workflow, not a claim that a given generation will preserve every product detail. If visual accuracy is essential, review the clip alongside the source before publishing.
