How to use Frase for an editorial brief you can fact-check

Follow one English query from search research to a four-section brief, then fix an actual attribution error before drafting.

Quick answer

Use Frase to collect questions and assemble an editable brief. In our November 11 trial, we exported a brief and a saved draft for a weekly content calendar. We identified a source credited to the wrong publisher and kept unverified keyword volumes out of the brief.

Frase Review

Define the reader and the market

Our example was “How to plan a weekly content calendar for a small creator team.” The tool initially suggested Chinese and China from our multilingual site. We set English (US) and United States before running research. Decide what the article will deliver: a weekly table with owners, deadlines and a publishing check.

Keep one query and one question in the brief

The research export contained 26 keyword rows, of which 21 had no volume available. We pinned one keyword and one question rather than treating all suggestions as proven demand. We chose How-To and set an 800-word target. The resulting brief had four editable sections. A practical structure is weekly capacity, a sample calendar, a pre-publish check and a Friday review.

Correct the publisher before quoting the number

One research entry labeled a 34% figure as HubSpot Research, but its link went to Hootsuite. The November 14, 2023 Hootsuite announcement attributes the finding to its 2024 Social Trends report. It concerned consumers’ attitudes toward brands’ self-promotion. If the article is about a small team’s calendar, the statistic may add little. Verify it first, then keep it only if it answers the reader’s question.

Export the brief and edit the claims before publishing

The Markdown brief retained audience, outline and sources. A generated draft with a weekly table persisted after reload, but its 70/30 capacity rule had no verified basis. Replace it with your team’s actual time budget or label it as an example. In a separate URL-import test, Improve scores twice reported a saved-draft version conflict. We retained the original article and applied no suggestions.

Decide what to repeat for the next article

Keep the verified source list and the four-part outline if those helped. Measure the minutes from first search to a usable brief, including fact-checking, before adopting it across the team. Results here are from one English US session. The trial did not measure search rankings or AI citations.

Judge the brief on one article before expanding

One Trustpilot user valued finding questions their competitors covered. Another found the workflow complicated and was unhappy with the drafts. Pick a topic you already know and judge the brief on three things: a precise reader question, an outline with a distinct angle, and sources you can open and verify. If the draft requires more checking and rewriting than your usual process, keep the useful research and write it yourself.
