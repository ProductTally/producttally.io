Frase for small publishers: research a topic, write a brief and edit the draft

Use Frase to turn one search question into an editorial brief, then verify claims and decide what to keep in the draft.

Quick answer

Frase fits publishers who want research, outlining and optimization in the same workspace. Its best role in this workflow is to give an editor a first pass to work from. Frase can bring English topic research, briefs and first drafts into one editorial workflow. In a single-user, seven-day no-card trial on 11 November 2026, the research-to-new-draft path worked. It suits publishers who will check sources and edit claims before publication.

Frase Review

Start with one research question

Choose the target language and market explicitly. For an English US content-calendar topic, Frase produced 26 keyword rows and an editable brief. We generated an approximately 800-word article, exported the Markdown and confirmed that the saved draft survived a reload. These observations do not establish ranking or citation gains.

Missing search volume needs another check

Volume was Unavailable for 21 of 26 keywords. Missing data is not zero demand. Rows showing $0 CPC or Easy difficulty alongside missing volume should not become a confident low-competition recommendation without further evidence.

Check the source, not just the statistic

One research item labeled a 34% statistic HubSpot while linking to Hootsuite. The original Hootsuite announcement supports the number in its 2024-report context; the publisher label needed correction. A source link does not make every surrounding claim accurate.

Read audit coverage before acting

The audit discovered 376 URLs but scored 19 pages. Discovery is not a completed audit of every page. A warning about missing section headings conflicted with the live homepage containing one H1 and five H2 headings; compare the actual page before changing markup. Scores are vendor diagnostics, not search-engine scores.

Keep an editor between the draft and publication

URL import retained the public guide and its tracking parameters. Improve scores then failed twice with saved-draft version conflicts; no suggested edit was applied. A newly generated article worked, but its 70/30 capacity split was unsupported as a general rule. Treat a ratio as a team-specific example or replace it. Automatic fact-checking and unattended publishing are not established.

Compare workspace costs and limits

Observed 11 November 2026: Starter USD49/workspace/month with 1 seat and 1 domain; Professional USD129 with 3 seats and 5 domains; Scale USD299 with 5 seats and 10 domains. Annual monthly equivalents are USD39/103/239, billed annually. These are workspace prices, not per-seat prices. Confirm taxes and final checkout terms. AI visibility, CMS publishing and Chinese generation were not tested.

Judge the brief on one article before expanding

One Trustpilot user valued finding questions their competitors covered. Another found the workflow complicated and was unhappy with the drafts. Pick a topic you already know and judge the brief on three things: a precise reader question, an outline with a distinct angle, and sources you can open and verify. If the draft requires more checking and rewriting than your usual process, keep the useful research and write it yourself.
