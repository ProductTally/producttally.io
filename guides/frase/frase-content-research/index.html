What is Frase for? A choice between research, briefs and drafting

Judge Frase on whether it gives your editor a usable angle and source list for a real article.

By Evan · Updated Nov 16, 2026

On this page

  1. Quick answer
  2. Try it when a writer starts without a brief
  3. Use a draft to expose gaps before paying for scale
  4. Test existing-page improvement separately

Quick answer

Frase is a research and drafting workspace for small publishing teams. Consider it if you repeatedly turn broad search topics into assignments for writers. Its strongest fit in our trial was a new brief with an editor checking the evidence. If you already know the topic and have the primary sources, writing directly may take less work.

Frase Review

Try it when a writer starts without a brief

A creator asks for an article about content calendars. Give Frase the reader, the market and the expected result. Then compare the suggested questions with two or three real search results. Keep the one gap you can fill with an original weekly calendar. A writer should receive an angle and a useful example, plus sources that actually support the story.

Use a draft to expose gaps before paying for scale

In our English US trial, a brief exported with its audience, headings and source list. One statistic in the research was misattributed, and a draft turned a proposed time split into a general rule. Assign an editor to check who published each claim, when and for which audience. If reviewing the draft takes longer than writing from the brief, keep the brief workflow.

Test existing-page improvement separately

Our new draft saved successfully. An imported article stayed intact, but Improve scores failed twice with a version conflict. If updating existing content is your reason to buy, try a low-risk page and check the resulting edits before upgrading. Check the workspace limits for articles, domains and seats on the current plan page.
