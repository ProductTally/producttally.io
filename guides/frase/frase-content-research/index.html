What is Frase for? Search research and editorial briefs

See where Frase fits for a small editorial team turning search questions into briefs and drafts.

By Evan · Updated Nov 11, 2026

On this page

  1. Quick answer
  2. Use the brief to choose an angle
  3. Judge the sources and the cost of editing
  4. Give Frase a defined role

Quick answer

Frase brings search research, outlines and draft optimization into one workspace. It fits publishers with a human editor who can judge which questions and sources belong in an article.

Frase Review

Use the brief to choose an angle

For a content calendar article, group reader questions around planning, examples or templates, then choose which reader need the article will answer. Keeping those questions next to the draft helps the writer see why each section belongs.

Judge the sources and the cost of editing

In our English research trial, many keyword rows lacked volume and one cited claim needed checking against its primary source. Trustpilot users described different experiences with topic discovery and generated drafts. The useful comparison is how much verification and rewriting your editor still has to do.

Give Frase a defined role

If your bottleneck is moving research into a usable brief, Frase is a candidate. For reliable keyword metrics or hands-off publishing, evaluate those needs separately. The related research guide explains what the trial produced and where it needed editorial judgment.
