Submagic for short videos: edit captions and choose an export workflow

Turn a selected clip into a captioned short. Check transcript corrections, SRT handoff, video export and credit use.

By Evan · Updated Nov 11, 2026

Quick answer

Submagic is most useful when you already have a short clip and want to style and correct its captions in a browser. Decide whether you need a subtitle file for another editor or a finished video. In our Submagic trial, we corrected captions and downloaded an SRT. Exporting the video with captions at 720p and 30 fps opened a paid plan selector.

Submagic Review

Start with a clip you can verify

Pick a short vertical clip with audio you can check word for word. Include a proper name, a number and a brief pause. If your regular footage includes two speakers or background noise, keep a second sample for that case. Use footage you have permission to upload.

Distinguish upload from a finished export

We uploaded the same 13.8-second vertical clip with synthetic English speech from two entry points. Both projects stayed at 98% for over five minutes and were editable on a later visit. Both rendered “Tally” as “L”. We corrected one, switched it to the Nora template and verified the correction in the downloaded SRT.

We did not measure completion time or general transcription accuracy. The video export prompt produced no rendered file. An SRT contains caption text and timing, so it does not verify styling in a rendered video.

Estimate credits using your actual edits

Submagic lists three credits per upload, including automatic captions. Under its published rates, adding silence removal and audio cleanup brings that example to seven credits. Its documentation lists no separate export credit charge. Trial access to video export is a separate subscription question.

Use one finished file as the buying test

Complete a sample in your publishing language and play the downloaded file on a phone. Check names, caption timing, safe margins, resolution and the final credit balance. Submagic is worth evaluating if that file meets your publishing standard within the time and credit budget you set.

Choose where your final video gets made

A creator on Reddit reported exports with sync and playback problems. For your next short, keep the source clip and corrected SRT. If you edit or mix audio elsewhere, finish there and check that the imported captions still line up. If Submagic is your final editor, play the downloaded video from start to finish before committing to a batch.
