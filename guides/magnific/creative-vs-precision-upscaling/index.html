How to upscale images in Magnific: Creative vs. Precision

Compare Creative and Precision in Magnific with one 2× image test. Check text, logos, and texture at delivery size before choosing a plan.

Quick answer

Start with Creative when a small illustration or 3D render could benefit from extra texture in a larger layout. Start with Precision when a product photo needs to retain its packaging, lettering, and colors. Magnific documents Creative as the mode for generated detail and Precision as the mode for closer reproduction. Inspect either output against the original.

Magnific Review

Choose a mode for your source image

Illustration or 3D render · Creative · Texture, outlines, and consistency with the original style

Product photo with packaging text · Precision · Lettering, logo shape, and color

Portrait for delivery · Precision · Facial features, hairline, and skin texture

Compare both modes on one image

1. Choose the delivery dimensions first. For example, 2× takes a 1200 × 800 pixel illustration to 2400 × 1600 pixels.

2. Open Image Upscaler in Magnific and upload an image you have permission to use. If both modes offer 2× for your selection, run one pass in each mode at that scale.

3. Start Creative with Subtle. Change one setting at a time if you need to refine it. Lower Creativity for less added texture or raise Resemblance to keep the result closer to the source.

4. Review both versions in the actual layout, then inspect two critical areas at 100%. If lettering changes or an edge looks artificial, switch versions or find a higher-resolution original.

When is a subscription worth considering?

Magnific is worth trying on a real project if you regularly need larger versions of small illustrations or renders. If ordinary resizing already looks good at the intended size, keep that workflow. Compare Premium, Plus, and Pro by the upscalers and credits you need, then check your local price and billing period.

Can Creative recover the original details from a blurry image?

Creative can generate plausible texture. For exact lettering, logos, or facial details, look for a clearer source and inspect the result against it.
