Quick answer

Predis.ai is worth trying for a small shop that makes recurring launch posts and already has approved product information and photos. Its Instagram carousel maker drafts editable slides, a caption and hashtags from a brief or product link. Judge it on how quickly you get an accurate, publishable post from the first draft.

Predis.ai Review

Start with the work you already do

Use the tool for repeat launches with a stable set of brand colors and type. If you produce one carefully art-directed campaign at a time, compare against duplicating an existing template. Keep a copy of your current workflow so you can measure time to an approved draft on the same brief.

Give the five slides a concrete brief

Fictional example: a moss-green 350 mL travel mug, photographed closed and in a commuter tote. Use five cards: launch photo, commute use, verified capacity and lid, maker-approved care instructions, and a clear next step. Hold the care card until you have approved instructions. Leave price, leak resistance and delivery claims out until you can verify them.

Make every claim traceable before export

Compare each draft with the product sheet. Reject a slide if the generated mug has a different lid, if 350 mL becomes 500 mL, or if the caption adds a discount that is missing from the brief. Swipe through on a phone and check that the last slide points readers to an actual clickable destination, such as the account’s profile link. The illustrated layout is an editorial example.

Record the effort and see if a plan fits

Time the first draft, fact check and one revision. Record credits before generation and after the revision. With one approved post, use the full credit difference as the cost of that post. With zero approvals, record the spend and the reason the draft failed. Compare your time and quality with the template workflow. Check the current plan before relying on automatic posting. Downloading the slides lets you post manually when needed.
