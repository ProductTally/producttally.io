Your Frase score went up. Does the opening tell the reader what to do?

Compare two openings about changing a published URL, then use Frase topic suggestions to find missing questions.

By Evan · Updated Nov 18, 2026

Use Frase's topic suggestions to find gaps after you have an answer. To test the opening, read it against the original query. Someone asking how to change a published article URL needs to know where the old address should lead and what to update. Frase's contextual analysis and topic groups can flag related questions worth covering.

Two openings with the same topic

  • Frase content optimization

Terms first. “URLs, SEO redirects, canonicals and sitemaps all need attention when changing an article address. Proper settings help the migration.”

Action first. “If the old article URL has already been shared, decide where it should lead. If you control server redirects, permanently redirect it to the new URL. Update internal links, its canonical and the sitemap. Then open the old link as a visitor to check the destination.”

  • Google Search site moves

The second opening gives the order of work and states the server access it assumes. If the host cannot provide that redirect, describe the available fallback later in the article.

Read the opening before checking the score

Give the opening alone to someone outside the editing process. Ask what they'd do first, what access they need and how they'd know it worked. If they hesitate, revise the opening. Then use Frase to spot missing questions you can answer with evidence elsewhere on the page.

For a single article with a replacement URL, map the old URL directly to its new equivalent. Where server redirects are available, Google recommends a permanent server-side redirect, such as 301 or 308. Verify the new URL and update its self-referencing canonical, internal links and sitemap. This is a publication check independent of the score.
