Choose a Udemy Photoshop course for product photo background removal

Use layer masks to remove a product photo background, check difficult edges and hand off both an editable PSD and a transparent PNG.

By Evan · Updated Nov 23, 2026

Choose a Udemy Photoshop course that combines subject selection, layer masks and export. For a product photo, the goal is to remove the background while giving the next editor room to restore a small edge or change the backdrop. A layer mask keeps the original image available and controls where it appears.

Udemy · View product details

Make a product photo you can still edit on Friday

Try a coffee mug with a curved handle and light glaze, photographed against a pale backdrop. The middle of the mug is easy to select. The inside of the handle and the pale edge are where a generic “remove background” lesson can leave you guessing. Use a photo you own or are allowed to edit. Make an initial selection, then add a layer mask. On that mask, black hides, white reveals and gray makes an area partially visible. Check that you are painting on the mask thumbnail before you touch the edge.

  • Adobe’s Photoshop layer-mask guide

Inspect the edge in two places

  1. Inside the handle. Put a colored fill layer underneath and look through the opening. Any strip of the original backdrop will be easier to spot.
  2. Around the outer contour. Toggle between a light and a dark temporary background. A pale rim can disappear on white and stand out on a dark shop page. Review at 100% for stray pixels and at the final display size for the overall silhouette.

Keep the layered PSD as your editable master. When you need a transparent PNG, hide the temporary backgrounds, enable transparency in the export where applicable, and open the exported file over a colored background. A white area in an exported file might be opaque white. Checking it with colored pixels behind it makes the difference visible.

Daniel Walter Scott’s Udemy *Adobe Photoshop CC – Essentials Training Course* lists a layer-mask lesson and an exercise using Select & Mask on difficult edges. Preview an available lesson and look for the moment when the instructor returns to a mask to adjust the selection. That step matters when the product image has to work on more than one background.
