Google Trends shows 0. Is the topic still worth a guide?

Widen the window, check the reader’s wording, and use other evidence to decide whether to publish a niche guide.

By Evan · Updated Nov 19, 2026

A zero in Google Trends Explore can mean there was too little search activity to report in your selection. The chart represents relative interest on a 0–100 scale. If a narrow question returns zero, keep the market and search type fixed and widen the time window before deciding whether the topic is useful.

Change only one setting at a time

Suppose you want to write about returning a secondhand camera bought from a small shop. Keep the same geography and Web Search setting. Start with the exact phrase over the past 90 days, then try the past 12 months. Next, running a separate comparison, broaden the wording to “used camera returns.” Record the dates and the exact terms. A new comparison can rescale the chart, so compare only series plotted together.

Use the broad term to find the decision the narrow guide should answer

Read the results for each phrase. A broad phrase may lead to store policies, while the narrower question calls for a checklist of what to check before buying. Keep the narrow article when you have a concrete case and enough reliable information to make that decision easier. If the same checklist already belongs in a guide you publish, expand that guide and keep its established URL.

For the editorial note, write down the broader term's chart settings and the exact reader question. Add a recurring support question or an impression from your own Search Console if you have one. Mark volume unverified. Review the page later using its actual query and country data. That gives you a reason to continue, update or fold it into another piece.
