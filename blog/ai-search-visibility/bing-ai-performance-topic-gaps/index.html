Two pages appear in the same Bing AI topic. Should you merge them?

Use Topics Portfolio to find the theme, then compare cited URLs and reader tasks before keeping, linking or merging the pages.

By Evan · Updated Nov 17, 2026

Start with what each page helps the reader do. Topics Portfolio is a beta view in Bing Webmaster Tools that may appear when the site has enough data. It groups grounding queries into themes and shows signals about each topic, existing queries and opportunities. To see which pages are cited for a particular query, check Citations & Pages, then read the two pages.

Use the topic to find the queries

  • Bing Topics Portfolio announcement
  • Bing AI Performance and page-level citations

Use Your Grounding Queries for terms where your site already appears. Opportunity Queries shows terms where other sites are cited and yours is absent.

Compare what each page actually answers

Fictional example comparing two booking guides
Example questionCited pageWhat the page helps the reader do
Why can't I book when one host is free?Group scheduling guideFind a slot when both hosts are available
The booking moved. Why can't the client see the new time?Rescheduling and cancellation guideVerify the updated invitation, email and calendar copy
How can I tell whether a booking notification arrived?Both pages could be citedRead both pages to see which offers a workable check

Keep the first two entry points because they address different stages of the booking. If both pages show up for the third question, read the cited context. When one briefly mentions email and the other explains how to check delivery across two time zones, link to the detailed explanation. Merge only after confirming that two pages in the same language solve the same task and that one lacks evidence or a use of its own. After a merge, document the URL treatment and check internal links.

Record the observation and decision separately

Treat the topic figures as directional. If the beta tab is missing, check existing pages from Citations & Pages. Two pages in the same topic may show up in different answers. Record the citation observation and the editorial decision separately.
