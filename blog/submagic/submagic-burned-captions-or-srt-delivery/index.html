Submagic burned-in captions or SRT? Deliver both from one interview

Choose a video export for feed playback and a separate subtitle file when editors need to update captions.

By Evan · Published Nov 27, 2026 · Updated Nov 27, 2026

Submagic supports captions burned into a video and SRT download. Use burned-in captions when the visual treatment should be part of the video. Use a separate SRT on a player that accepts it when viewers need subtitle controls or the publisher needs to correct text without replacing the video.

Keep one approved transcript

For a 15-second interview clip and its longer YouTube cut, check the speaker's name and any amounts against the transcript. Export each edit with its matching timing. The opening phrase in the long edit might start at 00:15 but be at 00:00 in the short, so give the SRT the same version name as its video.

  • Official documentation · www.submagic.co

Check each deliverable in its actual player

For the feed MP4, play a frame with a face and a lower-third to check placement. For the longer edit, attach the SRT on the destination and turn subtitles off and on to check alignment. Keep the approved text so you can revise the subtitle artifact later.
