How to share a Gamma proposal that clients can review

Use Gamma’s viewing and commenting permissions to get decisions on a proposal and keep internal pricing and notes in a separate file.

By Evan · Updated Nov 17, 2026

The client says, “Version two looks good.” You now have to ask whether they mean pricing option B or the deck you emailed yesterday. Gamma is useful here because you can keep the material the client needs in one shareable presentation and give the client permission to view or comment. The harder part is deciding what you want them to respond to.

Gamma · View product details

Ask for three decisions

For a website redesign, try putting a question under each relevant part of the proposal. Under scope, ask whether another language needs to ship at launch. Under deliverables, name who will supply the copy. On the timeline, ask for a date to approve the homepage. A good prompt is specific enough for the client to answer on the spot. “The estimate covers the English site. If Japanese needs to launch at the same time, please confirm here and name the person who will supply that copy.”

In Gamma, give editors permission to change the deck and give the client Comment access if you want feedback in the file. Use View when feedback belongs in a separate system. The workspace can grant its members broader access than the link setting suggests, so check both before assuming a colleague only has the ability to view.

Build a client copy with nothing extra inside

Keep your subcontractor rates and unapproved pricing options in an internal version. Share a copy that contains only the pages the client should see. Gamma applies sharing permissions across the whole file, including every page in a multipage gamma. If the material is confidential, use the access method your client requires. Gamma’s help center lists password protection for eligible docs on Pro and Ultra, with Gamma Sites excluded.

Open the link in a signed-out window to check what an uninvited visitor sees. If the client needs to comment, check that separately with an invited account. After the review, put the agreed scope, price and date in the record both parties use to approve the work. For your first pass, reuse an old proposal with fictional client details. Open it cold and see whether you can answer all three questions without asking for another attachment.
