Compare conflicting sources in Gemini Notebook (NotebookLM)

Compare conflicting sources in Gemini Notebook, formerly NotebookLM. Follow citations and check dates, units and scope using two public FCC reports.

By Evan · Updated Nov 19, 2026

On this page

  1. Give each source its own turn
  2. Open each citation in context
  3. Compare dates, scope and definitions
  4. Write a source-backed conclusion

Ask Gemini Notebook to cite each source separately, then check the date, scope and definition beside each figure. The US FCC used 25 Mbps download and 3 Mbps upload in its 2015 fixed broadband assessment. In March 2024 it announced a new benchmark of 100 Mbps download and 20 Mbps upload. These figures describe different historical benchmarks for US fixed broadband. They do not tell you what speed a particular household received.

Gemini Notebook · View product details

Give each source its own turn

  • GAO report on broadband speeds (2023, p. 5)
  • FCC 2024 Section 706 report (GovInfo, paragraphs 22–23)

Name the files by publisher and year, such as GAO 2023 and FCC 2024. Select the first source and ask: “What are the download and upload benchmarks? Who set them and what do they apply to? Cite the passage.” Repeat with the second source. Then select both and ask for a table with source date, scope, units and figures, plus any explanation the later document gives for the change.

Open each citation in context

Google documents citations that jump to the source passage. A very short source may be cited as a whole. If a PDF breaks a number across lines, search the original for a shorter phrase such as 25 Mbps and read around it. When the answer omits the year, check the cover or publication date.

Compare dates, scope and definitions

In the 2023 GAO report, look for “Since 2015, FCC has set this speed benchmark at 25/3 Mbps” on page 5. In the FCC’s 2024 report, paragraphs 22–23 explain the change. Even if a citation takes you to the right page, confirm that it supports the figure, the year and the fixed-broadband scope.

Write a source-backed conclusion

Report the two figures on separate lines and cite the matching document on each. Keep the historical US FCC scope. To answer what applies today, check the current official publication separately. This comparison was checked against public documents by hand. The prompts show how to reproduce the reading task in Gemini Notebook.
