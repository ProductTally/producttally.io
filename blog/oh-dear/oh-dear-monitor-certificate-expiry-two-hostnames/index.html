Monitor SSL certificate expiry for your main site and help center with Oh Dear

Set useful renewal lead times for two hostnames and distinguish expiry, hostname coverage and certificate-chain alerts.

By Evan · Published Nov 28, 2026 · Updated Nov 28, 2026

Set up an Oh Dear monitor for each public hostname whose certificate matters. With separate certificate health checks, you can give the main site and help center different warning thresholds and determine which host needs attention from the alert.

A green main site tells you little about the help subdomain

Picture a store on example.com and a help center on support.example.com, hosted by different providers. Record who can renew each certificate, who can change DNS and when that person usually needs notice. That makes “certificate expires soon” an action someone owns. If both hosts share a certificate, checking both still verifies the names they actually serve to visitors.

  • Certificate health

Choose a warning interval that fits the renewal process

Open each monitor's Certificate Health settings. Oh Dear documents default warnings within 7 days for Let's Encrypt and 14 days for other certificates, with a custom threshold from 1 to 100 days per monitor. A provider-managed main domain may fit the defaults. For an approval-heavy help center, a longer lead time lets you coordinate access before renewal becomes urgent.

  • Certificate health

Read the type of alert before changing DNS

An expiry warning is about renewal timing. A hostname mismatch means you should compare the intended public name with the names covered by the certificate. A chain warning sends you to the certificates served by the host. Keep your alert channel routed to someone who can access the relevant provider, and confirm the repaired public hostname after a renewal.
