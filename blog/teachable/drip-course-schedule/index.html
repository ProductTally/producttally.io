How to drip a course in Teachable by date or enrollment

Choose a Teachable drip schedule for a live cohort or rolling enrollment. See what unlocks, when it unlocks and when the email goes out.

By Evan · Updated Nov 17, 2026

On day one of a four-week editing course, the student only needs to cut 30 seconds of footage. If their first login also presents color management, export settings and delivery specs, they have to work out what matters today. Teachable’s Drip feature releases course sections on a calendar date or after a set number of days from enrollment. Pick the schedule by deciding who needs to stay in step.

Plan around the deadline students share

If everyone meets you for feedback on Fridays, choose dates. You can open the rough-cut assignment together and release the pacing lesson for the following week. Explain how late enrollment affects the live sessions. A student joining after the first feedback call needs to know whether a recording or another chance to submit work is available.

If enrollment stays open, use days after enrollment. Open with a footage pack and a short assignment, then make the pacing section available seven days after each student joins. A Monday signup and a Thursday signup have different release dates. In Curriculum, open Drip on the section and choose the date-based or enrollment-based option. Teachable schedules at the section level. Put a lesson in its own section if it needs a different release date.

A lesson can unlock hours before the email

Teachable’s documented release time is 00:00 UTC. With announcements enabled, the email goes out at 15:00 UTC. In New York during daylight saving time, a Friday release is available on Thursday at 8 p.m. and the email goes out Friday at 11 a.m. Eastern. Pick a date at least 48 hours ahead when you set a fixed-date release. Send your own reminder if the scheduled email would come too late for a live session.

Give students an unlocked place to find files and shortcuts they need throughout the course. Put lessons behind the schedule where the preceding assignment gives those lessons context. Preview those first few sections with a student account before building the full curriculum. If buyers need everything as an on-demand reference, you can keep the whole course open and still suggest a weekly study plan.
