Make your first podcast T-shirt with Printful and print it to order

Check one shirt for print quality and sizing, then price it with the costs of a delivered order.

By Evan · Updated Nov 17, 2026

On this page

  1. Make two previews and change only the shirt color
  2. Look at the lettering and measure the shirt you receive
  3. Price one delivered order before you launch
  4. Launch the version you checked

A listener says they would buy a shirt with your show’s running joke. That is a reason to make a sample. Printful lets you wait for orders before producing the shirts. You run the design, store, and pricing. Printful prints, packs, and ships paid orders it receives. Before you list the shirt, decide whether the real thing looks right and whether a sale leaves enough money.

Make two previews and change only the shirt color

Pick one blank in Design Maker. Put the same artwork you have rights to use in the same print area. Save the template and compare the design on a dark and a light shirt. Choose one combination to order so your first sample has a clear purpose.

Look at the lettering and measure the shirt you receive

Lay it flat, measure one critical dimension according to the product’s size chart, and stand back to see whether the smallest line reads clearly. Take a photo with a size reference for the listing. Change the lettering if it blurs or check the garment and size if the fit is off. The sample tells you about that specific combination.

  • Printful Design Maker

Price one delivered order before you launch

Printful separates the base product cost from shipping, taxes, and extras. Here is a worked example in dollars. A $26 sale less $14 for the printed shirt, $5 in shipping you cover, and $2 in store and payment costs leaves $5 before items such as taxes, discounts, or refunds. If shipping is $8, that falls to $2. Look up actual costs for your shirt and destination.

  • Printful pricing and order costs

Launch the version you checked

If the sample works and the sale leaves enough room, list that combination first. With a supported store such as Shopify or Etsy connected, check the synced variants, price, and buyer-facing shipping before promoting. Use actual orders to decide when to add a color.
