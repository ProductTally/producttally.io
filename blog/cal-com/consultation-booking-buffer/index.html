How to set Cal.com buffers for client consultations

Set a buffer, minimum notice and a daily limit in Cal.com. See what each rule changes on a client booking page.

By Evan · Updated Nov 17, 2026

At 10:30, the first client is still explaining what they need. The next one has already joined the waiting room. Even if you end the first call on time, you still need a few minutes to write down what you promised. Cal.com lets you set aside that time at the event level, alongside a minimum notice period and a cap on how often people can book.

Cal.com · View product details

Budget for the work around the call

Say you offer a 30-minute design consultation. Twenty more minutes gets you time to turn the conversation into notes and a follow-up email. Start with these sample settings and adjust them after a week of real calls.

Example consultation settings
SettingSample valueWhat the rule protects
After-event buffer20 minutesTime to record decisions before the next call
Minimum notice24 hoursTime to read the brief and look through the client's site
Booking frequencyTwo per dayA block in the afternoon for project work

A call booked from 10:00 to 10:30 needs room until 10:50 under this example. The next start time clients actually see also depends on your slot interval and connected calendar. A 24-hour notice rule handles a different problem. It hides times that start less than 24 hours after someone opens the page, even when your calendar is free. The daily frequency cap applies to this event type, so a separate follow-up link can still take bookings.

Give repeat clients a shorter way back in

A new client may need to send a brief. An existing client who wants to pick one of two layouts might only need a ten-minute call. If both use the same link, the 24-hour rule slows down the second person too. Give repeat clients a separate event type with the notice you need for that work. If you want both types of calls out of your afternoons, restrict their availability or protect that time on your connected calendar.

Before you share the link, open it as a visitor and look for three slots. One starts too soon. One bumps into a meeting or its buffer. One fits the rules and should still be bookable. If all three disappear, check your available hours and slot interval before changing the buffer.

Start with the one link clients already use. At the end of the week, look at what you did with the time between meetings. If it lets you send the follow-up before starting the next call, the buffer is doing its job.
