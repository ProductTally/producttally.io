When to recycle an evergreen post in Publer

Choose which posts to republish, set a stopping point and use Publer’s Recycling schedule without crowding out new material.

By Evan · Updated Nov 17, 2026

Months after you shared a tip on recording podcast interviews, the same question shows up in your replies. The tip could still help someone. Last month’s discount serves a different purpose. Once it expires, sharing it again sends people to an offer they can no longer use. Publer distinguishes Recycling for reusable posts from Recurring for time-bound repeats. Choose the schedule after checking what the reader will find when they click.

Publer · View product details

Rescue one useful post

Start by opening three old links. If a tutorial shows an obsolete screen or a download has moved, update the destination before sending more people there. Look for a tip readers can act on today. “Ask your guest to clap once before the interview so the sound spike helps you align the tracks” is a stronger candidate than a recap of an old promotion. Give it a new first line that tells the reader when they will need it.

Set an end date or a limit on the number of times the post can be recycled. Publer’s help center also documents an option to recycle only on days that have no scheduled posts. That lets you protect a planned launch while filling gaps elsewhere. Recycling takes effect on a later date and uses available slots, so use an ordinary scheduled post for something that has to go out today.

Source · Publer Help Center — What’s the difference between recycling and recurring posts?

Decide which result earns a place in next month’s schedule

For the recording tip, look for people opening the tutorial, saving it or replying with a follow-up question. Compare posts on the same account and at roughly similar times before drawing conclusions. Two quiet attempts might mean the opening is unclear or that followers have already seen the advice. Take it out of the queue and try a different problem before increasing the frequency.

This works best for teams with a useful back catalog and occasional gaps in their schedule. Keep dated offers and advice that changes with every release on a separate schedule. Start with one post and a stopping point. If it helps readers find something still worth reading, you have a reason to use the slot again.
