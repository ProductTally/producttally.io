Google says your video isn’t on a watch page. Should you make one?

Decide whether readers come to watch the video or to read the guide, then check the right URL in Search Console.

By Evan · Updated Nov 19, 2026

Google’s Video indexing report uses this reason when a video is secondary to its page. Give a video its own watch page when playing that one video is the main reason to visit. Keep a supplementary clip in the article when it helps readers understand a step. The article can still be eligible for ordinary text results.

Give the video a page only when it earns the visit

Imagine a six-minute workshop replay and a written guide with a 30-second excerpt. The reader of the guide may want written steps and a reference table. The visitor to the replay wants a player and enough context to start watching. Keep the excerpt in the guide, and use the full replay on a dedicated URL if people need that video on its own. Google allows the same video to appear on both a watch page and a supporting page if that serves readers.

  • Google video SEO guidance

Make the original player visible on load

For the watch page, keep the video prominent and make it available in the rendered page without requiring a click to insert the player. Give it a stable thumbnail URL and describe the actual video in the page copy and VideoObject data if you use it. Verify the page itself is indexable before looking for video indexing.

  • Google video SEO guidance

Read the report for the canonical watch URL

Inspect the watch page and check whether Google sees the video. If the report still treats it as secondary, open the rendered HTML and check what appears with the player. An article that intentionally uses a complementary clip can keep the message in its own inspection result. Record the watch URL and its video status separately from the article URL.
