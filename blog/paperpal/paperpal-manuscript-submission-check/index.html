How to review a Paperpal manuscript check before submitting to a journal

Review a Paperpal Journal Fit report alongside the target journal’s instructions before editing your manuscript or submission form.

By Evan · Published Nov 25, 2026 · Updated Nov 25, 2026

Use Paperpal’s Journal Fit report to find issues to review in a submission draft. Paperpal describes checks for structure, word counts, disclosures and author metadata, among other items. Open the target journal’s instructions beside the report and make each change in the manuscript or the submission form it belongs to.

Open two documents before you change the manuscript

Suppose you’re submitting a methods paper with your co-author. Open the author instructions for the exact journal and article type. Keep your submission copy next to those instructions. If your institution has rules about uploading unpublished work, follow those before sending a file to any external checker. If you can use Paperpal, upload the document to Journal Fit and open its suggested edits. Paperpal describes a downloadable version with highlights and comments.

Start with issues your team can resolve in the manuscript. If the report flags the abstract length, check the journal’s limit for this article type and trim the text where needed. If a funding disclosure is missing, go back to the grant record or ask the corresponding author. Only add a statement that the authors can confirm. A conflict-of-interest declaration needs the same treatment.

Give each open issue a place to go

A missing affiliation may belong in the manuscript, a separate title page or a submission field. If the instructions call for blinded review, check where identifying details belong before adding them. Put author names and funding statements into an author query when you need someone else’s confirmation. Fix the items you can verify now, then review the complete submission package together. The journal’s instructions guide the final choice for article-specific fields.
